How to fold it
  1. Print the sheet on a single piece of paper at actual size (100%, no “fit to page”), landscape.
  2. First crease. With the printed side facing outward, fold the sheet in half along its length, so the two long edges meet. Press the fold firmly, then open it again.
  3. Second crease. Fold it in half across its width, so the two short edges meet. Press firmly and open it again.
  4. Quarter creases. Bring each short end in to the centre crease, like closing a pair of shutters. Press, then open the sheet completely flat. You should see eight panels.
  5. Cut the slit. Fold it across the width once more. Starting from the folded edge, cut along the central crease for one panel’s length and stop there. Unfolded, that is the red line on the diagram, with both ends still joined.
  6. Open the slit. Unfold the sheet and fold it along its length again. Hold the two short ends and squeeze them toward each other: the slit stretches into a plus shape and the panels fan out.
  7. Close the booklet. Press everything flat so the front cover (page 1) is on top and the back cover (page 8) underneath.

Tip: do a dry run first with a plain sheet. Number the panels in pencil as in the diagram and check the pages end up in order before you print.

Paper and printing
  • Everyday choice: 24 lb / 90 gsm bright white copy or laser paper. It folds crisply and prints well. Thicker paper gets bulky where the layers meet at the centre.
  • Photos: matte inkjet paper around 28 lb / 105 gsm, or a lightweight matte photo paper. It holds ink better than copy paper and still folds.
  • Avoid: cardstock and anything heavier than about 32 lb / 120 gsm. Glossy photo paper is a poor choice too: the coating cracks at the folds and the ink dries slowly.
  • Thin paper (20 lb / 75 gsm) works, but ink can show through the back where pictures overlap in the folded stack.
  • Grain: most letter paper has its grain along the long edge, which suits the long-way fold. The short-way folds cross the grain and can look rough, so press each crease with a bone folder or the edge of a ruler.
  • Printer settings: actual size (100%, no “fit to page”), landscape, highest quality. Most printers cannot print to the paper edge, so use the Small or Medium page margin unless your printer does borderless.
  • Sizes: US Letter or A4 make a pocket zine. A3 makes a bigger one but needs a wide-format printer or a print shop.
  • First try: plain 20–24 lb copy paper and a pencil to number the panels, before printing the real thing.
